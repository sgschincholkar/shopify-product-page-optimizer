# PDP Conversion Rules

Evaluate the page around one primary conversion goal: helping a qualified shopper decide whether to add the product to cart or continue toward purchase.

## Priority order

1. Value proposition clarity.
2. Product and audience fit.
3. Benefits and differentiation.
4. Trust and proof.
5. Objection handling.
6. Purchase friction.
7. Scannability and media support.

## Value proposition

- A shopper should quickly understand what the product is, who it is for, what outcome it supports, and why it differs.
- Prefer a specific customer benefit over broad feature lists or clever slogans.
- Compare the merchant's positioning with competitor patterns, but do not manufacture differentiation.

## Benefits and decision information

- Translate verified features into benefits without extending beyond the evidence.
- Put high-value decision information early: use case, key differentiator, material or formulation, size, compatibility, and usage where applicable.
- Use 5-7 benefits-first bullets that remain concrete and scannable.
- Identify missing information that blocks a purchase decision rather than filling the gap with assumptions.

## Trust signals

- Inspect reviews, ratings, guarantees, returns, shipping, certifications, credentials, testing, sourcing, security, and contact transparency when present.
- Place relevant proof near the claim or decision it supports.
- Recommend a trust signal only as `proof required` unless merchant evidence confirms it.
- Competitor trust signals show category expectations; they do not authorize the merchant to make the same claim.

## Objection handling

- Derive likely objections from product complexity, price, category risk, merchant omissions, and recurring competitor FAQs.
- Cover fit, usage, compatibility, care, delivery, returns, safety, expected experience, and value only where relevant.
- Write 5-10 FAQs using supported answers. When an answer is unknown, turn it into an information request or claim warning instead of guessing.

## Friction and scannability

- Flag unclear variants, hidden price context, confusing purchase options, weak hierarchy, dense paragraphs, missing next-step information, and unsupported urgency.
- Recommend headings, bullets, comparison information, and image roles that reduce decision effort.
- Do not assess button placement, mobile usability, page speed, checkout behavior, or visual hierarchy unless rendered-page evidence is supplied.

## Recommendation format

Prioritize each meaningful gap by expected buyer impact and evidence strength. Separate:

- `observed`: directly present or absent in inspected evidence.
- `inferred`: a reasonable category pattern that still needs merchant validation.
- `recommended`: proposed copy or content change.
- `proof required`: a potentially valuable claim or trust marker the merchant must substantiate.
